   Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1236 AM CDT Tue May 25 2021

   Valid 251200Z - 261200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PARTS OF
   THE UPPER GREAT LAKES AND OVER PORTIONS OF THE CENTRAL/SOUTHERN
   PLAINS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated severe thunderstorms are forecast this afternoon/evening
   across parts of the upper Great Lakes region and across parts of the
   central and southern Plains. Hail and wind are the primary risks.

   ...Upper Great Lakes...

   25/00z short-range model guidance suggests a well-defined upper
   trough, currently located over SK, will shift east-southeast to a
   position from the MB/ON border into the eastern Dakotas by early
   evening. In response to this feature, a notable surface cyclone will
   translate across southern MB into northwestern ON by late afternoon
   with a trailing surface boundary expected to extend along the MN/WI
   border into northwestern IA. Latest models suggests strong
   boundary-layer heating will be noted across the upper MS Valley and
   convective temperatures will likely be breached by 20z within a
   strengthening westerly flow regime. It appears adequate low-level
   convergence and buoyancy will be present for isolated thunderstorm
   development initiating across northwestern ON/MN Arrowhead by
   20-21z. This activity should develop south across northwestern WI,
   possibly evolving into a broken squall line ahead of the upper
   trough. Strong deep-layer bulk shear and large-scale support suggest
   wind/hail may accompany this activity.


   ...Central/Southern Plains...

   Large-scale height field will remain weak across lower latitudes
   through the day1 period. Latest water-vapor imagery is not
   particularly helpful identifying any notable short-wave trough or
   disturbance that might be embedded upstream of the central/southern
   Plains early this morning. Even so, strong diabatic heating will
   prove instrumental in destabilization along the dry line across west
   TX into south-central KS. Forecast soundings suggest CINH will be
   negligible by 23z across this region and isolated thunderstorms are
   expected to develop where surface temperatures warm into the
   lower-mid 80s. While deep-layer flow will not be that strong,
   adequate high-level flow should support at least organized
   multi-cell clusters and perhaps a few weak supercells. Large CAPE
   values and steep lapse rates will prove favorable for hail/wind with
   these diurnally driven storms.

   ...Western NY/Northwestern PA...

   Strong heating is expected across western PA into western NY which
   will allow surface temperatures to warm to near 80F in the lee of
   Lake Erie. Although deep-layer flow will not be that strong, steep
   lapse rates and MLCAPE possibly approaching 1000 J/kg could support
   a few robust storms capable of generating some hail/wind. This
   activity will be diurnally driven and weaken downstream with the
   loss of daytime heating.

   ..Darrow/Wendt.. 05/25/2021
