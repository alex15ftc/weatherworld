About WeatherWorld

A storm-chasing world where the weather is simulated, shared by everyone, and forecast before it happens.

One engine, one sky

The weather here is not a set of scripted events. A weather engine steps a whole atmosphere forward: a jet stream and its troughs, fronts, a dryline, moisture coming north, daytime heating. Storms form where that atmosphere supports them, and what they become, from a brief shower to a long-lived tornadic supercell, follows from the air they grow in.

A single engine runs for every game server. Two players in different servers who drive to the same county see the same storm, at the same stage, at the same moment. The weather also carries on when nobody is playing.

What this site publishes

  • Convective outlooks for today and the next two days, made from an ensemble of forecast runs of the same engine.
  • Watches and mesoscale discussions as storms come together, and warnings for each storm that turns severe.
  • The analysis a forecaster would use: mesoanalysis and soundings, synoptic charts and model runs.

The products follow the form of the real ones because that is how forecasters read weather, and players use them the same way: to pick a target before the storms go up.

How the clock works

The world runs eight times faster than real time, so eight world days pass in each real day and a world day lasts three real hours. Dates on this site name the real date and which of its eight world days it is: "11/12/26 Day 8" is the eighth world day of 12 November 2026. Clock times are the world's own, in UTC and in local time (CDT).

Using these images

You are welcome to share the maps and loops from this site. Please keep the title line that says where they came from: these are graphics of a simulated world, and without their label an outlook map can be mistaken for a real forecast.

Not real weather

Nothing on this site describes the real atmosphere. It is not connected to NOAA, the National Weather Service or the Storm Prediction Center. For real severe weather information, use your national weather service.
