# Storm-lab visual techniques: reference vs. lab, and what to do in Roblox (2026-10-29)

Read-only research. I compared the lab captures (`docs/stormlab/detail/studio_soft_*`, `studio_tower_*`, `game_first_storm.png`; the tornado captures are from the older 10-23 renderer) with the Severity/Twisted frames and the real photo sheets. Scores are my judgement from the images. They are **uncertain** where the newest code (TornadoCards, Scud, the wall cloud and tails from 10-26) has no capture. `structure/after2/*.png` are radar plots, not visuals, so they aren't scored.

## 0. Findings that frame everything

- **Nobody has published how Severity, Twisted or Helicity render their storms.** No DevForum posts, devlogs or interviews turned up. The only public trail is Twisted's changelogs: cone meshes up to 1.16, "81 wall cloud/tornado mesh combinations" in 1.17, a "dynamic tornado system", RFD cuts and shelf clouds in 1.20, and a "lite" build for weak devices. Severity's listing warns it "will NOT run well on low end devices". Everything below about Severity is inferred from frames, mostly by the existing diagnosis in `docs/tornado-reference/NOTES.md`.
- **Every Severity reference is near-field, within about 1 km.** The turbine base clip, Ref1, the debris wedge and the mobile house clip are all close. At 1 stud = 0.28 m, the ~800-stud particle cutoff is only ~225 m, so Severity's big-particle look only covers what is that close. The references show nothing of a whole supercell at 5–20 km, so for the far view the real photos are the only target. Our billboards (no draw cutoff) are the right carrier there.
- **Roblox in 2026 has no native volumetrics or custom shaders.**
  - The community raymarchers (an April 2026 DevForum WIP, "Skibidi Clouds") are CPU EditableImage renderers with checkerboarding and no depth, or an external shader loader that Roblox disallows.
  - Terrain Clouds are still one global layer; the Nov 2025 request for cloud types has no staff reply.
  - This matches the lab's own CloudVolume verdict from 10-22, so it is not worth re-proposing.
- **Relevant engine facts:**
  - **SurfaceAppearance:** the map Content properties, including EditableImage maps, are PluginSecurity, so scripts can't set them. Color, EmissiveStrength and EmissiveTint are unrestricted in the metadata, but the page says "most properties cannot be modified by scripts". Test before relying on any of them.
  - **Beam:** has ZOffset (camera-relative shift for layering), LightEmission (0 normal to 1 additive), LightInfluence, Brightness up to 10000, FaceCamera, Segments, TextureSpeed and SetTextureOffset. Its docs say nothing about distance culling, so the lab's "textured beams far away don't draw" stays an empirical limit.
  - **ParticleEmitter:** has Squash, flipbooks (2x2, 4x4 or 8x8, Loop/OneShot/PingPong/Random, up to 30 fps) and FlipbookBlendFrames. There is no documented distance limit, but users report a cutoff around 1000 studs and caps of about 16k particles on PC and 3.6k on mobile/Xbox.
  - **EditableMesh/EditableImage:** client budgets are "strict" and device-dependent, with no published number. Users report ~6–8 meshes, matching our measurement.
- **The general-game techniques that map onto Roblox are the cheap ones:**
  - billboard/impostor clouds (what we do)
  - pre-lit sprite sheets picked or blended by light direction (Unity "6-way lighting"; our front/side/back lobe sheets are a 3-way version of it)
  - big tiled cloud layers that rotate slowly (Roblox's own *Mystery of Duvall Drive* storm: stacked, semi-transparent, tiled cloud meshes spinning at different speeds, lit by the scene)
  - Nubis/RDR2/MSFS-style raymarching does not map onto Roblox.

## 2. Techniques considered and where they land

| Technique | Fits Roblox? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Raymarched volumes (Nubis/HZD, RDR2, MSFS) | Only on the CPU into EditableImage, low resolution, no depth | No. Tried here on 10-22 and in the community; still WIP in 2026. |
| Billboard / impostor clouds | Yes (BillboardGui draws at any distance) | Current method; keep. |
| Pre-lit sprite sheets chosen by light direction (Unity 6-way lighting) | Partly: no per-pixel blend, so pick one sheet (our 3-way) or relight on the CPU into an EditableImage (the old VaporRelight: 1.1 ms per 256² redraw, budget-bound) | Keep the 3-way pick. A 6-way bake (adding top and bottom) would help undersides and mammatus. |
| Stacked tiled cloud layers rotating slowly (Duvall Drive) | Yes, with static uploaded meshes (no EditableMesh budget) | A candidate for the overhead deck and the anvil sheet, but "transparency looks glassy" was found here on 10-23; flat SurfaceGui quads (#1) are the cheaper test. |
| Big slow particles with fibrous flipbooks (Severity's near field) | Within ~200 m only | Use for dust and scud right round the camera only. |
| Camera-facing Beam stacks (Severity Ref1 funnels) | Yes, at near-to-mid range | Best match for smooth funnels; needs the user's OK (#11). |
| Soft particles / depth fade | Not available | Hide intersections by height fades. |

## 3. Ranked changes, by expected visual gain per effort

1. **Background-relative tone (#2):** lit rain and low cloud brighter than the base, scud darker than what's behind it. Code only. It is the core of the Severity look.
2. **Per-card aerial perspective (#15) plus a darker, wider base and core from afar (#17):** a few lines in StormCards' colour path; fixes the "pale tower on a blue sky" read.
3. **Continuous overhead deck (#1):** a few huge down-facing flat cards with the `ceiling` texture and a UIGradient. Removes the oval grid. Needs a yes on the "flat card" exception.
4. **Clear slot from the RFD field (#3):** depends on 3; high payoff, since it's the highest-contrast feature in real photos.
5. **Tower/base tonal tuning in the soft style (#6):** a `lobe_post.py` re-run (feather, warmth) and a darker sky-only underside. Cheap.
6. **Fibrous, layered, long-lived dust (#13):** existing sheets plus layout and lifetime changes; particles only inside ~200 m.
7. **Edge-on elongated laminar cards for striations and shelf tiers (#5, #10):** one crisp-edge sheet plus layout. Medium effort; creates a supercell signature we lack.
8. **Beam-core funnel hybrid (#11):** the best match to Ref1, but it needs the user's OK on a second primitive and a far-draw test. Before deciding, capture the current TornadoCards funnel; it may already be close.

Also cheap: a Bloom pulse and an in-cloud glow card for lightning (#14), and replacing the 5 Hz striation stepping with per-frame motion (#16).

**Before acting:** take fresh captures of the TornadoCards funnel, the wall cloud and tail, and the scud. Every score marked "?" is a guess about code nobody has looked at on screen.
