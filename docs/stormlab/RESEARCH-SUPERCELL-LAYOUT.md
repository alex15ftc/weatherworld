# Research: classic supercell layout, on radar and from the ground (2026-10-25)

This is for a right-moving supercell in the Northern Hemisphere. Bearings θ are measured clockwise from storm motion (0 front, +90 right flank, 180 rear, −90 left flank) and taken from the **low-level mesocyclone (LLM)**, which is also the tornado / occlusion point. Sizes in km.

Sources: NWS spotter guides; NWS Louisville SOO "Supercell Structure"; OU supercell radar signatures; Lemon & Doswell 1979; Markowski 2002 (hook echoes and RFDs); Burgess et al. 1982 (cycle timings); Penn State METEO 3. Some numbers are conceptual-model defaults, not measurements.

## Plan view (radar)

| Feature | θ from LLM | Distance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low-level updraft maximum | +45…+100 | 1–4 | spreads 5–10 km toward the inflow side; the LLM sits on its rear/upshear edge |
| Mid-level meso / BWER | −30…+30 | 2–6 | the updraft tilts downshear; the BWER is 3–10 km across at 3–8 km AGL, over the notch |
| Inflow notch / WER | +30…+120 | 2–8 | the gap between hook and core, opening to the right-front |
| Hail core | −100…−20 | 2–8 | hugs the updraft's forward-left edge |
| FFD main rain core | −60…+10 | 5–20 | 10–30 km long, downwind |
| RFD | +150…+240 | 1–5 | wraps cyclonically W → S → E of the LLM; the clear slot is on its W/SW side |
| Hook | curls from the core's rear-left through the rear to the right-rear of the LLM | arc radius 2–5 | 5–15 km long, 1–4 km wide, shallow (≤3–4 km); weaker than the core (40–50 vs 55–65 dBZ) |
| RFGF / FFGF | S–W / E | 5–30 | they meet at the occlusion point, at the LLM |
| Flanking line | +150…+210 | 5–30 | towers stepping up to the main tower along the RFGF |

## Tornado

- At the hook's tip, inside the curl, never in the main core.
- At or within 0–2 km of the occlusion point, on the RFD side.
- On the rear (upshear) edge of the low-level updraft, not under the updraft or BWER centre.
- At the centre of the velocity couplet.
- Several km (5–15) from the main rain core.

## Wall cloud

- Hangs from the rain-free base (RFB) right over the LLM.
- On the RFB's **rear edge, on the side nearest the rain**. It is not centred under the RFB, which reaches out toward the right flank and flanking line (RFB 8–20 km across, wall cloud 1.5–6 km).
- The tail cloud runs from it toward the forward-flank rain; the beaver tail runs along the FFGF to the main updraft.
- The clear slot is on its rear (W/SW) side and wraps S and E round it.
- The tornado forms under it, usually at its rear / right-rear edge by the clear slot.

## Cycle

1. **Occlusion:** the RFGF overtakes the FFGF, and the old LLM and tornado drift to the left-rear of the updraft into the cold air (the track hooks left).
2. **New meso:** a new one forms about 5–10 km right-front, along the RFGF/FFGF junction.
3. **Timing:** about 30 min to the first mature meso, later ones in about 10 min; each lives 45–90 min.
