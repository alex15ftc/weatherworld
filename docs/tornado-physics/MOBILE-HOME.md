# Mobile home: damage and debris

The single-wide mobile home is the first structure broken piece by piece. It uses EF-scale damage
indicator DI 3 (single-wide manufactured home).

- **Code:** `roblox/Shared/StormLab/Sim/MobileHome.luau`. It is pure Luau.
- **Geometry:** `roblox/Shared/StormLab/Structures/MobileHomePieces.luau`, generated by
  `tools/blender/mobile_home.py`.
- **Test:** `tools/stormlab/test_mobile_home.luau`.
- **Where it runs:** the StormLab town (`Labs/StormLab/Server/Town.luau`) builds its mobile-home
  park from the pieces.

This page records the science and where each number comes from.

## Where it came from

The asset began as a destructible-trailer kit: a Blender script, a skinned mesh with one bone per
piece, and a Roblox runtime. We kept these parts of it:

- **The pieces:** 128 rigid pieces, each one bone, at their real sizes and positions. The home is
  14 × 56 ft, with a 2.6 ft roof rise on 20° slopes, block piers at 8 ft spacing on two lines 7 ft
  apart, and optional skirting, porch, deck, shutters and cross gable.
- **The load path:** each piece lists the pieces that must be gone before it can go. A wall can't
  go while its siding, its windows and the ceiling it carries still stand. The full order is
  shingles, sheathing, trusses, ceiling, walls, floor, then chassis.
- **The server/client split:** each piece is an invisible proxy and the client moves the bones.
  This is for when the skinned mesh is uploaded. The lab builds the same boxes as parts for now.

We replaced these parts of it, because they were set by hand rather than derived from the fields:

| Kit | Now |
|---|---|
| Per-category thresholds on a 0–5 "intensity" | Each piece's failure speed comes from its DI 3 degree of damage (DOD). It is drawn per home, normal round the expected speed with σ = (upper − lower)/4, and never weaker than what covers it. |
| Radial falloff × height factor `0.55 + z/25` | The simulated wind is sampled at each piece. Capacities are 10-m open-country gusts, scaled to the piece's height by the ASCE 7 exposure-C gust profile, V ∝ z^(1/9.5) for z ≥ 4.6 m. |
| One load per piece | q = ½ρV² × the piece's net pressure coefficient, by its face and the wind angle (below). |
| Swirl/lift/inflow "target velocity" lerp | Sim/Debris: drag on the relative wind, lift on plates, gravity, ground friction. Each piece gets its own class from its geometry and material. |
| Unit motion: none (pieces only) | The unit slides, tips, rolls and lofts by force balance with friction and ground anchors (below). |

## Degrees of damage (DI 3)

`Damage.DI.mobileHome` now has all nine DODs:

- **DODs 1–6:** the US mph values (WSEC 2006).
- **DODs 7–9:** converted from the Canadian EF-scale guide's km/h table (ECCC 2013), which lists
  the same DODs. Its rows 1–6 agree with the US table.

| DOD | Damage | Exp. | Low–high (mph) | Breaks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Threshold of visible damage | 61 | 51–76 | vents, shutters, skirting, deck rails |
| 2 | Loss of shingles or partial uplift of the roof covering | 74 | 61–92 | shingles, cross-gable roof, windows, doors |
| 3 | Unit slides off its block piers, stays upright | 87 | 72–103 | *force balance*, plus porch, deck, steps |
| 4 | Complete uplift of the roof, most walls stand | 89 | 73–112 | sheathing, trusses, ceiling, gable face |
| 5 | Unit rolls on its side or upside down, intact | 98 | 84–114 | *force balance* |
| 6 | Roof and walls destroyed, floor and undercarriage left | 105 | 88–122 | siding, walls, interior walls |
| 7 | Unit rolls or vaults; roof and walls separate from the floor | 109 | 96–127 | *force balance* (lofted) |
| 8 | Undercarriage separates, rolls and is badly bent | 118 | 103–137 | floor |
| 9 | Complete destruction, debris blown away | 127 | 109–149 | chassis, piers |

DODs 3, 5 and 7 are things the whole unit does. They come from the force balance, not from a
drawn speed.

## Piece loads

The load on a piece is

    ratio = [C_net(face, wind angle) / C_ref] × V² / V_cap(z)²

and `Damage.accumulate` turns that ratio into damage. Duration counts: just above capacity, a
piece takes about 1.5 s to fail.

**Outside pressure coefficients** come from ASCE 7-16 for low-rise buildings. The home is
4.3 m tall on a 4.3 × 17 m plan.

- **Walls:** windward +0.8, side −0.7, leeward −0.5. A face's coefficient is fitted through these
  three as a function of c, the cosine between its outward normal and the upwind direction:
  Cp(c) = −0.7 + 0.65c + 0.85c².
- **Roof slopes:** −0.65 when the wind crosses the ridge, −0.9 when the wind runs along the ridge
  (the edge zone).
- **Updraft:** roof pieces add it, U² = u² + v² + 3w|w|, as the other structures do.

**Inside pressure** depends on whether the envelope is breached.

- **Sealed:** ±0.18, and the worse sign is taken.
- **Breached:** +0.55, once a window or door has gone. The breach changes loads in both
  directions. A windward wall's net load drops from |0.8 + 0.18| = 0.98 to |0.8 − 0.55| = 0.25.
  The roof's rises from 0.83–1.08 to 1.2–1.45, which is the old roof capacity × 0.87.

**Reference coefficient.** The DOD speeds stand for the first piece of a kind to fail, so they are
matched to the worst-loaded piece of that kind when sealed. That is C_ref = 0.98 for walls and
1.08 for the roof. Other pieces of the same kind need more wind.

## The unit: force balance

The unit is whatever is still attached. The piers, porch, deck and steps stand on their own. Its
properties come from the pieces:

| Property | Value | How |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | 6.9 t | sum of the pieces; a 14 × 56 single-wide is 6.5–8 t |
| Broadside area | 77 m² | half the summed projections of the outer envelope (each face pair counted once); inner walls count once their siding is gone |
| End area | 21 m² | same method |
| Plan area under uplift | 86 m² | roof plan, or the ceiling's once the roof is gone |
| Side-load centre | 2.2 m | area-weighted height of the envelope |
| Centre of mass | 2.4 m | mass-weighted height of the pieces |

**Areal masses.** These are typical building-material values, not measured on one home.

| Piece | kg/m² |
|---|---|
| Asphalt 3-tab shingles | 10.5 |
| 7/16" OSB sheathing (plus fascia) | 7.5 |
| 5/16" gypsum ceiling and insulation | 7 |
| Siding | 2.5 |
| Stud wall with panelling | 11 |
| Window | 22 |
| Floor (particleboard on joists) | 22 |

Steel members are by length: the chassis is 42 kg/m and trusses 7 kg/m. Hollow CMU piers are
about 900 kg/m³ of the stack.

**Forces on the unit.**

- **Side force:** F = q · 1.3 · A_side, where 1.3 is windward +0.8 plus leeward 0.5.
- **Uplift:** L = q · CL · A_plan.
  - CL = 0.7 on skirted piers or on the ground.
  - CL = 0.9 on open piers, because wind gets under the floor. Inside pressure cancels between the
    ceiling and the floor, so it adds nothing to the unit's uplift.
- **Friction:** μ = 0.55 for steel beams on shims and CMU, and 0.6 for the floor on soil.
- **Anchors:** 6 a side, each 21 kN ultimate. That is 4,725 lb: 24 CFR 3280.306's 3,150 lb
  working load plus the 50 % overload. They are diagonal ties at 45°, holding 0.71 across and
  0.71 up. Each home has a share of that strength (`anchors`, 0–1) for loose, rusted or
  wet-soil ties.

**Motion, integrated each step.**

- **Slide:** when F > μ(W − L) plus the anchors' share across, the ties let go. Kinetic friction
  then works against the motion. Once the beams ride 0.9 ft across or 8 ft along, the unit drops
  off the pier tops: DOD 3.
- **Tip:** when the side force's moment about the leeward support (the pier line, or the floor's
  edge on the ground) beats (W − L) × arm plus the windward ties, the unit turns about that
  corner, with I = m(w² + h²)/3. Each time the next corner lands, the unit keeps 45 % of its spin
  and turns about that corner. At 80° it counts as rolled: DOD 5.
- **Loft:** when the uplift beats the weight and the ties, the unit flies with drag and gravity
  until it lands: DOD 7.

**Wind needed to move it.** These are steady broadside gusts at 10 m, open country, from
`MobileHome.slideSpeed` / `tipSpeed` on piers:

| Anchor strength | Slides at | Tips at |
|---|---|---|
| 0 % (unanchored) | 54 mph | 55 mph |
| 30 % | 85 mph | 73 mph |
| 60 % | 107 mph | 88 mph |
| 100 % (to code) | 131 mph | 105 mph |

The DI 3 ranges for DOD 3 (72–103) and DOD 5 (84–114) are the span from loose to
code anchoring. An unanchored unit moves below the indicator's low end, as damage surveys of unanchored homes
report (they often fail at EF0). The test's ramp across 60 homes with anchoring spread 0–1
gives a median of 75 mph for rolling. That is below DOD 5's range because a third of the park is
barely tied.

## Debris

**A piece's own debris class** (`mh:<piece>` in `Debris.CLASSES`, registered by Debris itself so
server and clients fly identical pieces):

- **Mass:** from the piece's material.
- **Drag area:** its largest face times its solidity. Trusses are 0.2, rails 0.35, the chassis
  0.15.
- **Drag and lift:** flat plates take Cd 1.2 and lift up to 0.8. Compact parts take Cd 1.05 and
  no lift.
- **Size:** the piece's own box.

**Coverings tear into their units.** They don't fly as one sheet:

| Covering | Tears into |
|---|---|
| Shingles | tabs (`shingle`, 1.3 kg) |
| Sheathing, cross-gable roof | 4 × 8 ft sheets (`plywood`, 18 kg) |
| Siding, skirting | panels (`sheetMetal`) |

The count is the face over one unit's area, at most 12 per piece; the rest stays as unseen litter.

**How far things fly.** The Tachikawa number K = ρU²A / (2mg) sorts it. These are runs of 20 s
in a 50 m/s wind (`test_mobile_home.luau`, part 4):

| Debris | K | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Shingle tab | 38 | ~800 m |
| Shutter | 42 | ~800 m |
| OSB sheet | 24 | ~790 m |
| Truss | 3.8 | ~530 m |
| Floor section | 6.7 | ~650 m |
| Chassis | 1.2 | slides about 230 m along the ground at ≤ 13 m/s; never lofted |

That chassis result is the "rolls, tumbles and is badly bent" of DOD 8.

## Checked

`tools/stormlab/test_mobile_home.luau` (run with Lune 0.10, `lune run tools/stormlab/test_mobile_home.luau -v`):

- **Mass and areas:** in range.
- **Debris classes:** every piece has one.
- **Force balance:** unanchored moves below DOD 3's low end; fully anchored moves inside DODs 3–5.
- **Wind ramp:** broadside, exposure-C profile, 0.5 m/s per s, 60 homes. The median first wind of
  every piece degree (DODs 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 9) falls inside DI 3's bounds. No piece fails before
  what covers it.
- **Debris:** plates fly far, and the chassis is not lofted.
