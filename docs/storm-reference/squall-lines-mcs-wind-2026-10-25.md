# Squall lines, MCSs and damaging wind: literature numbers (2026-10-25)

This was gathered for the engine's line and wind work. **[abstract]** means only the abstract or a summary was read.
Unmarked figures were read in full text.

## Upscale growth
- **Dial, Racy and Thompson 2010.** The best predictor is the boundary-normal 850-300 mb mean wind, measured relative
  to the boundary.
  - Under 6 m/s storms stay on the boundary and grow into lines; above 6 m/s they stay discrete.
  - Ground-relative, the threshold is 10.5 m/s.
  - Cloud-layer shear normal to the boundary under 8 m/s favours lines.
- **Coniglio, Hwang and Stensrud 2010.** Fast-developing MCSs reach genesis 5 h or less after the first storms; slow ones
  take 7 h or more. The fast group had:
  - a stronger low-level jet closer to the first storms;
  - larger CAPE and DCAPE;
  - weaker 3-10 km shear.
- **Feng et al. 2019.** Upscale growth peaks about 4 h in. Great Plains genesis is often near sunset.

## Cold pools
- **Engerer, Stensrud and Coniglio 2008** [abstract]. Oklahoma Mesonet MCS cold pools:

  | Stage | Surface deficit | Pressure rise |
  |---|---|---|
  | First storms | 9.5 K (over 11 K by day) | 3.2 hPa |
  | Mature | | 4.5 hPa |
  | Dissipating | 5.4 K | 3.3 hPa |

  Gusts averaged over 15 m/s at every stage.
- **Bryan and Parker 2010** [abstract]. VORTEX2 squall line: cold pool about 4 km deep, C about 35 m/s, much larger than
  the shear.
- **Rotunno, Klemp and Weisman 1988; Weisman and Rotunno 2004.** Lines are strongest at C/du of about 1. When C is much
  larger than du the updraughts tilt upshear; non-optimal lines can still be long-lived (Coniglio et al. 2012).

## Motion
- **Corfidi et al. 1996; Corfidi 2003.**
  - Mean cloud-layer wind V_cl = (V850 + V700 + V500 + V300)/4.
  - Back-building motion = V_cl - V_LLJ.
  - Forward-propagating motion = 2 V_cl - V_LLJ.
  - Speed is underestimated when the system has a rear-inflow jet or supercells.
- **Cohen et al. 2007.** About 90% of derechos move faster than 18 m/s; most weak MCSs move slower.
- **Feng et al. 2019.** Plains MCSs move at about 18-20 m/s on average, most at 6-30 m/s.

## Updraughts, regeneration and lifetime
- **Updraughts.** Squall-line cores routinely exceed 15 m/s, with about 25 m/s in one MC3E case; supercells reach 36-50
  (Giangrande et al. 2013; Jensen et al. 2016; Marinescu et al. 2020) [abstracts].
- **Regeneration.** A new gust-front cell every 9-13 min in 2-D runs (Lin and Joyce 2001).
- **Lifetime.** Plains MCSs mostly last 10-24 h, mode about 15 h; they are at least 100 km long (Feng et al. 2019).

## Frequency
- **Feng et al. 2019.** Southern Plains, May: about 12 long-lived intense MCSs a month.
- **Haberlie and Ashley 2019.** Eastern US: about 111 MCSs a month in June.
- **Estimate (not from a paper):** an MCS in a region like ours on about 30-40% of May-June days.

## Damaging wind
- **Edwards, Allen and Carbin 2018.** 2006-2015: 6.3% of estimated and 8.8% of measured severe gusts were 65 kt or more.
  About 10% of reports were measured.
- **Smith et al. 2013.** Measured severe gusts by storm type: QLCS 42%, disorganised 47%, supercell 11%.
- **Downbursts.** Realised gusts are well below sqrt(2 DCAPE), by up to a factor of two (Gilmore and Wicker 1998, via
  Pryor 2016).
- **Weisman 1993.** Long-lived bows need CAPE of 2,000 or more and low-level shear of 20 m/s or more.
  - The rear-inflow jet is over 20 m/s (relative to the system) when mature.
  - Near-surface winds at the apex are 35-45 m/s.
  - Systems speed up from 18 to 29 m/s.
- **Evans and Doswell 2001; Cohen et al. 2007.**
  - Derecho environments: 0-1 km inflow relative to the system of about 20 m/s or more.
  - 2-6 km lapse rate 7.3 C/km.
  - Strongly forced cases have strong mean wind; weakly forced ones have high DCAPE.
