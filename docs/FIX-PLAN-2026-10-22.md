## 1. Hail size and hail outlooks

**Causes (measured).**

- Size: `js/storms/StormHazards.js:57-65`.
  - 2.0 · (w/60)² · rotation · lapse, then melting. There is no cap; the largest seen is 5.5 in.
  - The curve is conservative: 1.4 in at 50 m/s.
  - The lapse factor is below 1 under 8 C/km.
  - Supercells take in about half the region's peak CAPE (seed 404: median MLCAPE in their inflow
    1,142 J/kg while the map peaked at 3,400).
- Reports: only towns within 5 mi of the storm's **centre** report hail (`js/reports/stormLog.js`),
  and every report carries the core size.
  - Seed 2112 day 2 had 8,767 km of ≥1 in core track and 107 reports: 1.2 per 100 km, where real
    dense days give 5-15.
- Outlooks never read reports. Both the forecast members and the verification take their truth from
  the engine's hazard field (`js/verification/truth.js:38-40`: the core stone at the storm's centre
  cell).

**Fix, in order.**

- **A. Report model only.** No effect on any outlook and no refit. Hail falls in a swath whose stone
  shrinks from the core size to 1 in at the edge, and each town reports the size where it is. Add
  rural and road spotters scaled by population. This gives more reports and a realistic spread of
  sizes, but not bigger maxima.
- **B. Steeper size curve** (power 2.5-3, still 1 in near 42 m/s), plus why storms take in low CAPE.
  - Maxima go to 3.5-4.7 in.
  - On its own this raises the share of cores reaching 2 in from 15% to 31-50%. Hail hatching would
    then turn many more days Moderate. Not acceptable alone.
- **C. Verify hail and wind as reports, as the SPC does.** Build the outlook truth (members and
  verification) from the same report model; tornadoes stay as surveyed.
  - The calibration then maps the physical chance onto the reported chance.
  - Hatching follows the share of reports reaching 2 in, which stays near the real 10-15% even with
    big cores.
  - This is how the outlooks stay SPC-conservative while the storms are realistic.
  - Cost: a refit of CALIBRATION and CIG_SHARE.

**Recommendation:** A now; B with C later, with one refit for everything (see Order below).

**Wind.** Severe gusts come out of a formula with no intermittency and no dependence on storm type.
At DCAPE 900 and 70 km/h motion, every mature storm is about 60 mph. Report spots are laid every
25 mi along lines.

- Fix: a narrow surge or downburst swath in the report model, and lower constant gust shares.
- This changes the wind truth, so it goes into the same refit.

## 2. Regional surface pressure

**Main cause (measured).** The region slides along the large-scale model (up to 15 m/s east-west
and 8 m/s north-south). Regional air masses are carried by the wind without taking that slide off
(`RegionalAtmosphere.js:668` advect; slide at :700-701). The large-scale air moves over the map at
u - S, but the regional air is dragged along with the map.

- Old cold air sits under warm large-scale air. `coldDome`/`thermalBalance` (:498, :525-529) read
  that as a 4.5 km cold dome and add 7-8 hPa.
- That makes the warm 1030 hPa highs (8181 day 1, 31 day 2). The same lag left warm air behind a
  cold front, so pressure was lower behind it (8181 day 3).
- With the slide taken off, regional air follows its large-scale air within 1 K and the false highs
  fall 6-9 hPa.

**Other causes (measured).**

- Dome depth switches to 4,500 m whenever no warm cell is on the map; the first warm cell collapses
  all the domes. This caused the 11 hPa drop at 06-09Z (seed 77), not the slide's north-south
  terms, which are consistent.
- The 992 hPa low at the west corner (404 day 2) is lee trough + plateau warmth + heating stacked:
  -11.8 hPa on top of the large-scale -7.7.
- The first-hours jump on seed 2112 is real large-scale weather (+44 kt even with steering off),
  doubled by the day's -15 m/s slide.

**Fixes and what they change.**

1. **Advect in the region's frame:** use u - slide, v - slideNorth for every carried field.
   - Pressure: high confidence.
   - Warm sectors 5-9 K warmer on west-slide days: more CAPE, weaker cap, higher LCLs.
   - Fronts and drylines cross the map up to about 30 mph faster on strong-slide days.
   - Storms should move in the same frame, which also changes storm speeds for chasers.
   - Less false backed flow and SRH in the false domes.
   - Tornadoes and High/Moderate days likely go up, so retune.
   - Alternative: cap the slide speed at about 6 m/s, which shrinks both the error and the slide's
     usefulness.
2. **Continuous dome depth:** min(slope · distance, deficit / 3.5 K per km). Removes the jumps and
   trims arctic highs about 5 hPa. Steadier low-level jet and SRH.
3. **Stop lee + plateau stacking:** together at most about 5 hPa. Weaker western-edge low, less
   High Plains southerly flow and low-level jet, fewer western starts.

## 3. Front labels

Display only: no storm or physics code reads the lines (checked). The fields underneath do not
flicker; the analysis rules do (`js/atmosphere/BoundaryAnalysis.js`).

**Causes.**

- A veto deletes a front whose moist side is cool. That deleted the warm front by the 983 hPa low,
  and a stronger hidden crest knocked out the real cold front 80-160 km behind it.
- Kind comes from the line's half-hour motion, but positions are quantised to 8-16 km, which is 4-9
  m/s of noise against a 2 m/s threshold.
- Drylines and outflows have no memory; ids are just "type-N", so nothing is tracked.
- The dryline "moist side east" rule drops E-W moisture edges.

**Fix.**

- (a) Type a line by what changes across it: density contrast makes a front, kind by motion; a
  moisture edge with little density contrast is a dryline. Remove the veto.
- (b) Apply the exclusion zones only to crests that survive.
- (c) Track lines from frame to frame and keep ids. Change kind only after 2+ analyses agree, or
  use a ~2 h average of motion. Use "moist side not west" instead of "east".
- (d) Keep a tracked line down to 0.7 of the threshold.

**Effects.**

- Radar fine lines: stable ids also stop their patches reshuffling.
- The Roblox map and analysis overlays.
- Outlook and MD wording: more warm and stationary fronts near lows, fewer "dryline".
- No effect on storms, tornadoes or outlook numbers.

## 4. Storm type, initiation, night storms, tornado strength

**Causes (measured; 40-day baseline on seeds 1-10 x 4 days).**

- **(a) Multicells in deep shear.** The mid-level mesocyclone is
  0.35·ramp(shear06, 20, 55) + 0.65·ramp(SRH, 50, 200) (`StormRotation.js`), with SRH taken over
  0-1 km for surface-fed storms. With 0-1 km SRH near 55, deep shear alone gives a mesocyclone of
  about 0.2, so the storm stays a multicell.
- **(d) Tornado strength is mostly chance.**
  - Rank correlation of the random strength draw with peak wind: 0.81.
  - The two EF4s had draws of 0.92 and 0.98 in STP 2.
  - Evening storms are weak because the outflow factor falls from 0.57 (09 local) to 0.28-0.34
    (15-19 local) as cold pools grow (DCAPE ~1000); the cloud-base term and the evening 0-1 km SRH
    drop add to that.
- **(b) Night storms.**
  - Under a 50-60 kt jet the elevated MUCIN is 170-350 J/kg, while elevated lift is capped at
    50-65 J/kg.
  - The cap cools only under the large-scale ascent (`capLift`). Isentropic lift and jet
    convergence never erode it.
  - Two step gates (MLCIN > 75, MUCAPE > SBCAPE + 50) also apply.
- **(c) Initiation.**
  - Lift is nearly on/off: 30-100 J/kg as soon as w passes about 0.01 m/s.
  - The start rate ignores how far the lift exceeds the CIN.
  - So an uncapped day (31 day 3, CIN median 8) fires everywhere. Seed 2112 day 1 was in fact capped
    (CIN 250-350) with descending air.

**Fixes, with tested effects** (relative to that baseline: 3.7 tornadoes a day, tornado days 65%,
EF2+ 7%, 15-21 local share 35%).

1. **`ROTATION.shearShare` 0.35 -> 0.55.**
   - Multicells in ≥50 kt shear 26% -> 15%.
   - Tornadoes +14%, tornado days 73%.
   - 15-21 local share 40%.
2. **`outflowBalance` [1.0, 1.8] -> about [1.4, 2.4].**
   - 15-21 local share 43%, night share 26% -> 20%.
   - Tornadoes +83% before compensation.
3. **`INTENSITY` from the environment** (base 10, rotation 60, tail 0.6, STP 55).
   - Draw correlation 0.81 -> 0.65.
   - EF2+ now mostly in strong environments.
4. **Compensate with `TORNADO.fullRatePerHour`** (tested 0.4: 1-3 together gave 4.65 a day, tornado
   days 68%, EF2+ 10%, 15-21 local share 41%). About 0.33 should restore today's count.
5. **Initiation, not tested.** Make lift smooth in w, and scale the start rate by how far lift
   exceeds the CIN. Fewer starts on uncapped days, more along boundaries.
6. **Night storms, not tested.** Isentropic lift and jet convergence also cool the cap (`capLift`);
   replace the elevated-path step gates with continuous weights. Tornadoes about unchanged; more
   overnight hail.

**Keeping the rates.** After each group, run a ~40-day server-stepped audit
(scripts/activity-report.mjs, ~5 min) and move only `fullRatePerHour`. Refit the outlook calibration
once, at the end.

## Closed upper lows, negative tilt, triple points

**Measured (QG model alone, 60 days).**

- Closed 500 mb lows in a US-sized window about 1% of the time, none lasting 48 h. Real: most
  spring days have one near the US.
- Negative tilt is as common as positive (median 0°); only 5-6% of strong troughs are negatively
  tilted.

**Why.**

- The mean wind is the same at every latitude, so there are no slack-flow flanks for lows to close
  off in.
- There is no jet, so nothing favours negative tilt (real negative tilt comes from the jet's
  poleward shear).
- Damping, beta, deformation radius and a mountain ridge are not the cause (all tested).

**Options, tested.**

- **Lower both QG mean winds by 4-6 m/s** (e.g. 19.5/9.5).
  - Because the model is exactly Galilean-invariant, the systems are the same, only slower.
  - 6-10 times more closed lows, occasional cutoffs.
  - Systems at 9-11 m/s instead of 15, nearer to real; storms 8-12 kt slower (better for chasing).
  - It changes the regional parts that depend on the ground wind (the lee trough, the westerly
    downslope that builds the elevated mixed layer, dryline position, low-level SRH).
  - Steering's `systemSpeedMs` becomes ~10.
  - Needs a severe re-audit and an outlook refit.
  - Does not change tilt.
- **A north-south jet in the mean state.**
  - Gives the real tilt pattern: 22-32% negative on the poleward side, 2-3% on the equatorward side.
  - But it weakens the systems, needs a shear retune, latitude-aware steering and new code. It is a
    separate project.

**Triple points** (40 days at 21Z, measured).

| Feature | Share of days |
|---|---|
| Closed L on the map | 8% |
| Warm or stationary front | 25% |
| Dryline | 68% |
| Dryline end within 150 km of a front | 10% |
| That junction with an L nearby | 0% |

Real spring Plains tornado days with a developing low have one perhaps a third to a half of the
time (estimate). Causes:

- QG lows are weak and broad.
- The regional pressure rises 6-13 hPa eastward across the map, so an L rarely closes on the map.
- The warm-front threshold (5 K per 100 km) is above typical real warm fronts.
- The front veto above removes them.

## Jet stream prototype (2026-10-23, large-scale model alone, nothing changed in the engine)

**Method.** The background west wind of each layer becomes U(y) = U0 + dU cos(2 pi (y - Ly/2) / Ly):
the strongest at the domain's middle latitude, where the region sits, and slack at the flanks. It
wraps north-south. The vorticity gradient gains the jet's own terms (k^2 dU_i +- F (dU1 - dU2)),
and the jet is added in the advection and the gradient term. The script is a scratch subclass:
job tmp a0d03ff3 lows/qgstats.mjs (jetB = strength in both layers, jetS = the extra shear between
them at the core). The base winds are lowered by jetB, so the core keeps about today's winds.

- Negative tilt is not raised by any setting: it is within noise between seeds.
